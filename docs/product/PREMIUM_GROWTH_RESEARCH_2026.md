# Premium Growth Mechanics Research — 2026

Research-only. No product code touched. Purpose: study how consumer freemium apps
(dating, language learning) convert free → paid, extract the underlying MECHANIC
(not the product), and judge what translates ethically to LawMind given the
non-negotiable constraint: **verified citations, adverse-treatment/overruled
warnings, primary source text, and safety warnings can never be paywalled,
blurred, or used as scarcity bait.** Only automation, synthesis, monitoring,
workflow-depth, and collaboration may be charged for.

Every claim below is tagged **[VERIFIED]** (confirmed against an official/primary
source), **[VENDOR CLAIM]** (a company's own stated figure, not independently
audited), or **[UNVERIFIED — could not confirm]**. Nothing is stated as fact
without one of these tags.

## 1. Tinder

**SOURCE**: Official Tinder Help Center — [Priority Likes](https://www.help.tinder.com/hc/en-us/articles/360046802931-Priority-Likes), [Boost](https://www.help.tinder.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004506186-Boost), [Super Boost](https://www.help.tinder.com/hc/en-us/articles/360029087891-Super-Boost), [Super Like](https://www.help.tinder.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004493543-Super-Like); [tinder.com/faq](https://tinder.com/faq); [tinder.com/feature/platinum](https://tinder.com/feature/platinum/)

**MECHANIC**: Free users swipe blind — they cannot see who liked them. Gold adds
"Likes You": a real, already-computed list/grid of people who swiped right on
them, visible only on upgrade. **[VERIFIED]** Platinum adds Priority Likes —
paid users' likes are inserted earlier in the recipient's card stack and given
priority placement in the recipient's own Likes-You feed, plus the ability to
message a match before they've swiped back. **[VERIFIED, per help.tinder.com]**
Boost (a la carte, available to all) puts a profile in the top slot for 30
minutes for ~10x views; Super Boost (subscribers only) claims up to 100x.
**[VERIFIED]** Super Like flags a specific like as elevated interest; 1 free/month
for everyone, more for Gold/Platinum. **[VERIFIED]**

**WHY IT CONVERTS**: Loss-aversion + curiosity gap. The app tells the free user
concrete, real information exists ("N people like you") but withholds only the
*identity* — not a fabricated number. The conversion trigger fires at the exact
moment of proven, personalized demand (someone already said yes) rather than on
a generic upsell screen. Priority Likes and Boost monetize *visibility timing*,
a scarce, real resource (attention/impressions), not fabricated scarcity.

**WHAT TRANSLATES TO LAWMIND**: The pattern worth stealing is "real count,
already computed, gate the *depth* not the *existence*." E.g., a saved matter
already has N supporting and M contrary authorities found in the last
citation-network run — LawMind can show that real count (verified state intact,
all citations still fully visible if the advocate asks) and gate only the
*synthesized argument map / auto-updating monitoring of new contrary rulings*
behind Pro. The "priority" mechanic (Boost/Priority Likes) maps to workflow
prioritization: e.g., Pro-tier briefings compute first / draft generation runs
with priority queue position ahead of free-tier jobs during load — a real
operational resource (compute priority), not a manufactured one.

**WHAT MUST NOT BE COPIED**: Never blur or withhold an actual citation, an
overruled/set-aside warning, or a safety flag to create the "curiosity gap" —
that is exactly the class of information PD-forbidden from paywalling. Never
fabricate a "someone already found this" signal. Never make identity/existence
of a *verified* authority itself the withheld thing — only automation/synthesis
around it.

## 2. Hinge

**SOURCE**: Official Hinge Help Center — [Subscription and Purchase Benefits](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/38014282744595-Subscription-and-Purchase-Benefits), [Subscriber Experience](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/36311764345107-Subscriber-Experience), [What is the difference between Standouts and HingeX?](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/13721759938835-What-is-the-difference-between-Standouts-and-HingeX) (WebFetch on these pages returned HTTP 403 directly — content below is drawn from Google's indexed summary of the same official pages, cross-checked across two independent search results; treat article-level exact wording as **[UNVERIFIED — could not confirm by direct fetch]**, structure as **[VERIFIED via search-indexed official-domain content]**)

**MECHANIC**: All users, free included, get the "Standouts" feed — profiles
getting above-average attention, ranked by shared preference/recent activity.
This is NOT paywalled. HingeX (top tier) adds three things on top of Hinge+:
Enhanced Recommendations, Skip the Line (a continuous, always-on profile boost
for the life of the subscription, not a one-off consumable), and Priority
Likes. Match Group's Q2 2026 earnings call disclosed Hinge is *testing an
additional subscription tier in Q3 2026* aimed specifically at women, described
as wanting offerings "new, differentiated, and highly relevant" — i.e.
deliberately segmenting the paywall by user need rather than one-size-fits-all.
**[VERIFIED — Motley Fool transcript, Match Group Q2 2026 call, fool.com]**

**WHY IT CONVERTS**: Two separate levers, kept distinct on purpose: (1) a
free-tier *quality* feature (Standouts, given to everyone) that builds trust and
proves the algorithm works, vs (2) a paid-tier *visibility/speed* feature (Skip
the Line, Priority Likes) that monetizes how fast/often you're shown, not
whether the underlying matching logic works at all. Segmenting a new tier by
demonstrated demographic need (women's stated pain points) rather than a generic
"more" tier is a second, distinct lever.

**WHAT TRANSLATES TO LAWMIND**: The Standouts pattern — a genuinely good free
feature that is never paywalled, used to build trust in the core product — maps
directly onto LawMind's founder constraint: citation verification, primary text,
and safety warnings staying free IS the Standouts move, not a concession. The
"always-on continuous boost, not a one-off" (Skip the Line) maps to persistent
background monitoring: e.g., "watch this matter for new contrary rulings /
overruling events" running continuously for Pro users vs. a free user having to
manually re-search. The demographic-segmented-tier idea maps to a practice-area-
specific Pro tier (e.g., a criminal-litigation bundle vs. a civil-litigation
bundle) if usage data later shows the needs genuinely diverge.

**WHAT MUST NOT BE COPIED**: Nothing structurally manipulative was found in
Hinge's public mechanic itself — the concerning pattern lives in third-party
commentary (e.g. "Is Hinge Hiding Better Matches Behind a Paywall?" — a
third-party investigative piece, not an official Hinge statement) alleging match
*quality itself*, not just speed, may be suppressed for free users. That
allegation is **[UNVERIFIED — third-party claim, not confirmed by Hinge]** and
is exactly the shape of thing LawMind must never do even accidentally: paid
tier must never receive a *better-quality* verification pass or a *more
complete* citation set than free — only more automation/synthesis on top of an
identical verified base.

## 3. Bumble

**SOURCE**: Official Bumble pages — [Premium and Premium+](https://bumble.com/en-us/the-buzz/bumble-premium), [Bumble Boost](https://bumble.com/en-us/help/what-is-bumble-boost), [SuperSwipe](https://bumble.com/features/superswipe/), [Spotlight](https://bumble.com/en-us/the-buzz/bumble-spotlight)

**MECHANIC**: Beeline (who-liked-you) exists for every user, free included —
but for free users the photos are shown **blurred**; identity is only revealed
on Boost/Premium. **[VERIFIED — official bumble.com/the-buzz content, corroborated
across multiple independent search snippets referencing the same source]**
Spotlight: one free-tier-purchasable/premium-included weekly boost, 30 minutes,
moves profile to top of the queue; best-time-to-use guidance (6–10pm) is given
openly by Bumble itself, not hidden. SuperSwipe: consumable, 5/week on Premium,
10/week on Premium+, marks the swipe as elevated interest and is visible to the
recipient as "this person SuperSwiped you." **[VERIFIED]**

**WHY IT CONVERTS**: The blur is the purest version of "partial value reveal" in
the set — the data (someone liked you) is proven to exist and even visually
present, but the one specific piece of information that makes it actionable
(identity) is withheld. It differs from Tinder's version only in degree — a
visual tease rather than a locked list.

**WHAT TRANSLATES TO LAWMIND**: This is the mechanic the founder is most likely
thinking of, and it is also the one requiring the most care. A defensible,
ethical analogue: a free user's citation search can show "3 supporting and 2
contrary authorities found" with full case names, full verification badges, and
full primary text all visible (never blurred) — the Pro-gated layer is the
*argument-map synthesis* connecting them to the matter's specific facts, or a
generated brief section built from them. The number and the underlying verified
data are never obscured; only the labor-saving synthesis is paid. A la carte
SuperSwipe → a consumable "priority draft" or "priority ingest" credit (e.g.
rush-processing a document upload ahead of the queue) is a legitimate, real,
metered-resource analogue.

**WHAT MUST NOT BE COPIED**: The blur itself, applied to anything covered by
LawMind's founder-declared free-forever list, is categorically forbidden — this
is the single most direct violation risk in the whole research set. Blurring a
citation, a case name, an overruled-status badge, or safety-relevant text to
create desire would be indistinguishable from silently dropping or
misrepresenting a citation, which PRODUCT_CLAUDE.md §2 treats as company-ending.
Do not adopt blur, ever, for anything in the citation/verification/safety path.

## 4. Match Group (portfolio-level)

**SOURCE**: Match Group Q2 2026 earnings call transcript — [Motley Fool, fool.com, 11 Aug 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/11/match-group-mtch-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); corroborating coverage: [Investing.com transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-match-group-posts-q2-2026-eps-beat-shares-tumble-after-hours-93CH-4836126)

**MECHANIC**: Match Group explicitly tracks and discloses "payer penetration"
(% of MAU paying via subscription OR a la carte) and "direct revenue per MAU" as
its core monetization KPIs — **[VERIFIED — company's own stated metric names on
an earnings call]**, up YoY globally and across Tinder's top-5 revenue countries
in Q2 2026, with direct revenue per MAU +6% YoY globally. **[VENDOR CLAIM — self-
reported company metric, not independently audited, though earnings-call
statements carry SEC disclosure obligations]** The company runs subscription
(recurring, tiered — Plus/Gold/Platinum) and a la carte (Boost, Super Like,
consumable) as two deliberately separate revenue engines rather than one
undifferentiated paywall, and is actively testing new tiers per-app (Hinge Q3
2026 test) rather than a single portfolio-wide pricing model.

**WHY IT CONVERTS**: Splitting recurring (subscription, for users who use the
app constantly) from transactional (a la carte, for users who want a one-time
edge in a specific moment) captures both a low-intent-but-frequent user and a
high-intent-single-moment user with two different price structures, instead of
forcing both into one plan.

**WHAT TRANSLATES TO LAWMIND**: Directly supports a hybrid model: a recurring
Pro subscription for advocates who work matters continuously (drafting,
monitoring, collaboration) PLUS a consumable/credit product for occasional needs
— e.g., a solo practitioner who only occasionally needs a rush briefing or a
Hindi translation pass could buy a one-off credit instead of subscribing. This
also matches Apple/Google's own policy split (§7–8 below): subscriptions must
carry *sustained* value; one-off value must legally be an in-app product, not a
subscription — so the two-engine Match Group pattern is not just good growth
practice, it is closer to platform-compliant by construction.

**WHAT MUST NOT BE COPIED**: No evidence found of a specific manipulative Match
Group corporate practice beyond what's covered per-app above; nothing further
to flag at the portfolio level from this search.

## 5. Duolingo

**SOURCE**: Duolingo Q2 2026 earnings call transcripts — [Motley Fool, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/duolingo-duol-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-duolingo-beats-q2-2026-estimates-shares-sink-93CH-4839616)

**MECHANIC**: ~10% of MAU pay (Super/Max tiers) — **[VENDOR CLAIM — company's
own disclosed figure on an earnings call, SEC-disclosure-obligated but not
independently audited]**. Duolingo's own data: 7-day-streak users convert to
paid at 3–4x the rate of less-engaged users. **[VENDOR CLAIM, same basis]**
Management has repeatedly and explicitly chosen to hold back monetization
intensity in favor of user growth, stating new users don't monetize immediately
under freemium and treating that lag as an accepted tradeoff rather than a
problem to solve with a harder paywall. Current product move: testing a
"Super Lite" ad-supported tier as a *third* step between free and full paid,
not a binary. AI inference cost collapse (from $0.30 to <$0.01 per Video Call
by moving to open-source models) directly funded rolling a compute-expensive
feature (AI speaking practice) out to paid tier only — i.e., the paywall
boundary was set by real marginal cost, not by desire alone. **[VERIFIED —
company's own stated cost figures and rationale on the earnings call]**

**WHY IT CONVERTS**: Habit/streak formation is the product; monetization is
downstream of and gated by proven engagement, not by blocking access. The
premium tier sells convenience/removal-of-friction (no ads, unlimited hearts/
lives) and compute-expensive features (AI conversation), never sells "the
actual lesson content" — the core learning path stays free indefinitely.

**WHAT TRANSLATES TO LAWMIND**: Strongest direct analogue of the whole set,
because Duolingo's boundary — "free = the actual substance; paid = friction
removal + compute-expensive AI features" — is structurally identical to
LawMind's founder constraint of "free = verified citations/safety; paid =
automation/synthesis." Two concrete transferable ideas: (1) gate features by
**genuine marginal compute cost**, the way Duolingo gated AI voice calls only
once cost data justified it — e.g., a full multi-document argument-synthesis
call is expensive; a citation lookup is cheap, so the cost structure itself,
not manufactured scarcity, should drive what's free vs Pro. (2) Use *engagement*
(matters actively worked, documents drafted) rather than a hard day-count as a
trigger to *surface* an upgrade prompt — prompting a highly-engaged advocate
who is clearly getting value, rather than blocking a light user cold.

**WHAT MUST NOT BE COPIED**: Nothing manipulative found; Duolingo's own
disclosed strategy is closer to a positive model than a cautionary one here.
The one caveat: Duolingo can safely gate "content depth" (extra lesson units)
behind paywall because wrong/missing content has low stakes (a delayed lesson).
LawMind cannot use the same depth-gating instinct on anything where missing
depth could mean a missed defense or an unnoticed overruling — the analogy
breaks exactly at the safety boundary and must not be extended past it.

## 6. RevenueCat — State of Subscription Apps 2026

**SOURCE**: [State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps) · [Hard paywall vs. freemium: lessons from a 75% LTV lift](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/hard-paywall-vs-freemium) · [The right trial length isn't 7 days](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/7-day-trial-subscription-app) · [The State of Subscription Apps in 10 minutes](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)

**MECHANIC / DATA** (RevenueCat's own report, based on 115,000+ apps, $16B+
tracked revenue, 1B+ transactions across iOS/Android/web, primarily 2025
performance data — **[VENDOR CLAIM — RevenueCat's own aggregated platform data,
methodology/sample breakdown by app category not published in the fetched
pages]**):
- Hard paywall (no free tier at all) median Day-35 download→paid conversion:
  **10.7%**, vs freemium **2.1%** — roughly 5x.
- Hard paywall revenue-per-install at Day 60: **$3.09** vs freemium **$0.38**
  — roughly 8x.
- After 1 year, retention converges: freemium **28%** vs hard paywall **27%** —
  i.e. the initial conversion gap does not persist as a quality-of-user gap.
- ~50% of all paid conversions happen on Day 0 — first-impression paywall
  design matters disproportionately.
- Trial length: 17–32 day trials show a 42.5% median trial-to-paid conversion
  vs 25.5% for trials ≤4 days — but 55% of 3-day-trial cancellations happen on
  Day 0–1 vs 31% for 30-day trials, and the article's own caveat: **conversion
  rate alone is a vanity metric** — a user converting Day 7 and churning Day 8
  is a "hollow victory"; retained-revenue is the real measure.
  Category note: gaming and photo/video apps favor short trials (fast
  time-to-value); health/fitness, education, and travel favor 5–9 day trials.
  Headspace specifically **[VENDOR CLAIM, reported secondhand by RevenueCat, not
  independently confirmed against a Headspace-published source]** A/B-tested
  7/14/30-day trials and found 14 days won on annual plan — "long enough to
  feel low-risk, not so long intent decays."
- One RevenueCat customer case: freemium-with-multistep-paywall redesign
  produced a **75% LTV lift** in one case and a **50% subscriber-conversion
  drop** in another attempted-similar transition — RevenueCat's own framing is
  that the freemium-vs-hard-paywall choice is highly context-dependent, not a
  universal winner.
- Guidance on when each model fits: hard paywall favors bootstrapped/cash-
  constrained builders and products solving an acute immediate problem; freemium
  favors products with strong retention, network effects, or user-generated
  content, or apps hitting a paid-acquisition ceiling.

**WHY IT CONVERTS**: Not a single mechanic — this is the meta-lesson that the
mechanism (blur, priority, streak) matters less than *when* the paywall is
shown relative to demonstrated intent, and that trial length is a legibility/
risk-perception lever, not a "more is always better" one.

**WHAT TRANSLATES TO LAWMIND**: LawMind is not a network-effect or UGC product
and does not fit RevenueCat's "freemium favored" profile on those two criteria
— but it does fit "acute immediate problem" (a hearing tomorrow, a filing
deadline) which RevenueCat associates with hard-paywall performance. However,
LawMind's own non-negotiable (citations/safety free forever) makes a true hard
paywall impossible by design — the founder constraint already overrides what
would otherwise be the data-favored model. The actionable takeaways instead:
(a) trial length should be chosen for time-to-value, not defaulted to 7 days —
an advocate needs to actually run a real matter through the product to feel the
value, arguing for a trial nearer 14 days than 3; (b) design the Day-0
experience deliberately, since that's where roughly half of conversions and a
majority of trial abandonments happen; (c) treat conversion rate alone as
insufficient — track Pro-tier retained usage (matters worked, drafts finished),
not just signups, mirroring RevenueCat's own "hollow victory" warning; (d) a
subscription+consumable hybrid (per Match Group above) is consistent with
RevenueCat's broader 2026 report finding that hybrid monetization is spreading
beyond gaming into utility/productivity categories — **[UNVERIFIED — this
specific cross-category claim about hybrid-model spread was referenced in
secondary summaries of the report (SaaStr, Subscription Insider) but not
directly confirmed by re-fetching the primary RevenueCat page in this
research pass]**.

**WHAT MUST NOT BE COPIED**: RevenueCat's data is descriptive of what converts
best on average, not prescriptive of what's ethical for a safety-critical
professional tool — the 5x hard-paywall conversion advantage is exactly the
kind of number that could tempt someone to gate citation verification itself.
That must not happen regardless of what the data says converts better; the
founder constraint sits above the growth-optimization data, not beside it.

## 7. Apple App Store Review Guidelines

**SOURCE**: [App Review Guidelines §3.1 In-App Purchase / §3.1.2 Subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) — fetched directly, 23 Aug 2026. **[VERIFIED — primary source, exact quoted text]**

**MECHANIC (the rules, quoted)**:
- **3.1.2(a)**: *"If you offer an auto-renewable subscription, you must provide
  ongoing value to the customer, and the subscription period must last at least
  seven days and be available across all of the user's devices."* Permitted
  subscription content examples include SaaS and "apps that offer consistent,
  substantive updates."
- Subscriptions may be offered **alongside** a la carte purchases (Apple's own
  example: subscribe to a film library, or rent/buy a single film) — directly
  supporting a hybrid subscription+consumable design.
- *"Apps that attempt to scam users will be removed from the App Store. This
  includes apps that attempt to trick users into purchasing a subscription
  under false pretenses or engage in bait-and-switch and scam practices."*
- **3.1.2(c) Subscription Information**: *"Before asking a customer to
  subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price.
  How many issues per month? How much cloud storage? What kind of access to
  your service?"* — and must comply with Schedule 2 of the Developer Program
  License Agreement (pricing/duration/auto-renewal terms).
- **3.1.1**: any credits/in-game-currency style consumable sold via IAP **may
  not expire** — directly relevant if LawMind ever sells "credits" (e.g. rush-
  processing credits) rather than a pure subscription.
- Non-subscription free-trial mechanics (a "XX-day Trial" non-consumable IAP)
  must clearly identify, *before the trial starts*, its duration, what becomes
  inaccessible when it ends, and any downstream charges.

**WHY IT CONVERTS / relevance**: Not a growth mechanic — a hard legal boundary.
Included because it directly bounds what LawMind's premium design can look
like on iOS.

**WHAT TRANSLATES TO LAWMIND**: Design constraint, not inspiration: (1) any
consumable credit product must not expire; (2) a subscription must be paired
with a plain-language "what you get" description before the paywall is shown —
"unlimited AI-synthesized argument maps, priority draft queue, matter
monitoring" is compliant phrasing; vague claims are not; (3) a trial, if used,
must state upfront exactly what becomes inaccessible at trial end — meaning
LawMind must be precise that citations/safety remain accessible forever while
only synthesis features lapse, which is both a compliance requirement and a
direct expression of the founder's ethical line — the two point the same way
here.

**WHAT MUST NOT BE COPIED**: N/A — this section is the constraint, not a
mechanic to select from. The one MUST-AVOID: any dark-pattern subscription flow
of the kind Apple explicitly calls "bait-and-switch" or "false pretenses" —
i.e., never advertise a feature as included and then reveal it's Pro-only
after payment, and never design a paywall screen that obscures price/duration.

## 8. Google Play Billing Policy

**SOURCE**: [Understanding subscriptions — Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533?hl=en) — fetched directly, 23 Aug 2026 **[VERIFIED — primary source, exact quoted text]**; [Overview of one-time products](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16430488?hl=en); [Understanding Google Play's Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818?hl=en)

**MECHANIC (the rules, quoted)**:
- *"You must be transparent about your offer. This includes clearly and
  explicitly disclosing your offer terms, the cost of your subscription, the
  frequency of your billing cycle, the automatic renewal terms, whether a
  subscription is required to use the app, and any other material information
  about the subscription."*
- *"Subscriptions must provide sustained or recurring value to users throughout
  the life of the subscription, and may not be used to offer what are
  effectively one-time benefits to users (for example, SKUs that provide lump
  sum in-app credits/currency, or single-use game boosters). Products that do
  not offer sustained and recurring value must use an in-app product instead of
  a subscription product."* — i.e. a one-off "rush this draft" credit must be
  sold as a one-time product, never disguised as a subscription tier.
- Pre-enrollment: developers must *"clearly and accurately describe the terms
  of your offer, including the duration, pricing, and description of
  accessible content or services"* and disclose *"how and when a free trial
  will convert to a paid subscription, how much the paid subscription will
  cost, and how a user can cancel."*
- Explicit deceptive-UI prohibitions: no *"multiple screens in the purchase
  flow that lead users into accidentally clicking the subscribe button"*, and
  dismiss/close options must be clear so a user can tell they're not forced to
  subscribe to proceed.
- Google separately maintains a hard rule against *"deceptive or manipulative
  purchase experiences."*

**WHY IT CONVERTS / relevance**: Same category as Apple — a legal boundary, not
a growth tactic, but it also *forces* the subscription-vs-consumable split that
Match Group and Apple both independently arrived at for business reasons —
i.e., all three sources converge on the same structural answer from different
directions (policy, product economics, platform economics).

**WHAT TRANSLATES TO LAWMIND**: (1) A one-off "priority processing" or "extra
translation pass" credit must be built and sold as a Google Play one-time
product, never bundled into the subscription description as if recurring; (2)
the trial-conversion disclosure requirement means any trial UI must state, in
plain terms and before signup, the exact price and exact date of first charge
— this is compatible with, and should be designed alongside, the Apple
requirement above so one paywall screen satisfies both; (3) the explicit ban on
purchase-flow screens that trick a tap into a subscribe action rules out any
funnel design that relies on ambiguity to convert.

**WHAT MUST NOT BE COPIED**: N/A — constraint, not inspiration. Same MUST-AVOID
as Apple: no pre-selected paid plan disguised as the default/continue button,
no charge before disclosed trial-end date, no subscription framing for what is
actually a one-time benefit.

## Synthesis — what LawMind should actually take from this

**Strongest candidate mechanics, ranked:**

1. **Real-count, gated-synthesis (Tinder "Likes You" + Bumble Beeline count,
   minus the blur)** — show the true, already-computed number of supporting/
   contrary authorities and the full verified citations for free; gate only
   the labor-saving synthesis (argument map, drafted brief section) built on
   top of them behind Pro. This is the one mechanic that directly answers the
   founder's original prompt while staying inside the constraint, because the
   thing withheld is synthesis effort, never data or verification.

2. **Always-on monitoring as the paid layer (Hinge's Skip the Line: continuous,
   not a one-off consumable)** — a matter's citation-network watch that runs
   continuously and proactively alerts on a new contrary ruling or overruling
   event is a premium *convenience/automation* feature; the underlying
   overruled-status badge itself stays free and mandatory everywhere per
   PRODUCT_CLAUDE.md §6.

3. **Cost-justified feature gating (Duolingo: paywall set by real marginal
   compute cost, not manufactured scarcity)** — the most defensible principle
   in the whole set. Gate what is expensive to generate (multi-document
   synthesis, full argument maps, AI drafting) and keep free what is cheap and
   safety-critical (a citation lookup, a verification check). This also
   happens to be the same line Apple/Google implicitly reward, since it
   produces genuinely "ongoing value" (3.1.2(a)) rather than a thin wrapper.

4. **Subscription + consumable hybrid (Match Group's payer-penetration model,
   independently required by both Apple 3.1.2(a) and Google's "sustained value"
   rule)** — recurring Pro for advocates working matters continuously, plus
   one-time non-expiring credits (Apple 3.1.1) for occasional rush-processing
   or translation needs, sold as an explicit one-time product per Google's
   policy, never disguised as subscription.

**Platform constraints most directly bounding the design:**
- **Disclosure before purchase** (Apple 3.1.2(c), Google's pre-enrollment
  requirement) — any paywall screen must plainly state what's unlocked, price,
  and renewal terms before asking for payment; this is also just good practice
  given LawMind's trust requirement.
- **No misleading paywalls / no bait-and-switch** (Apple's explicit scam-app
  removal clause, Google's deceptive-UI prohibitions) — directly forecloses any
  version of the blur/tease mechanic applied to anything the founder has
  already promised stays free.
- **Consumable/credit semantics** (Apple: credits never expire; Google: a
  one-time benefit must be sold as a one-time product, not a subscription) —
  binding on the exact hybrid model recommended in item 4 above; any credit
  system LawMind builds must be structured to satisfy both from day one rather
