## 1 · THE FAILURE PATTERN — it has a name, a measured rate, and a known fix

The founder's words: *"You seem to tell me wrong things, and then you correct
yourself later."* This is not a quirk of one lane. It is the most-studied failure
mode in agent research, and the numbers are worse than the impression.

### What it is called

**False success** — an agent asserting completion when the environment state says
otherwise — and **premature commitment** — editing before it has gathered enough
evidence to justify the edit.

### How common

| finding | source |
| --- | --- |
| **44–52%** of all agent failures in single-control domains are false successes | [Confident Closing to Silent Failure](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09863) |
| **75.8%** of failures in coding-agent architectures that emit an explicit completion signal | ibid. |
| GPT-5 submits a patch on **100%** of runs and resolves **44%** | ibid. |
| Reasoning models give **no protection** — traces *"rationalize completion rather than verify it"* | ibid. |

**That last line is the one that matters.** Thinking harder does not fix this.
Only touching the environment does.

### What actually works — and what does not

| mitigation | effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Dual control — a second party with independent verification** | false success falls to **3%**, from 44–52%. An order of magnitude, and the largest single effect in the literature |
| **Structured evidence conditions** compiled before an edit is allowed (ECLoop) | **+4.8 to +11.8 pp** pass@1 across two models and two scaffolds, and **−12.1%** tokens |
| Cheap mechanical detectors | recover **72%** of false successes at a 10% flag rate |
| An **LLM judge** doing the same job | recovers **13%**. Also **3,300× slower** (4,000 ms vs 1.19 ms) |

Sources: [ECLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28815) ·
[False Success](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09863) ·
[Preventing agents declaring victory early](https://walkinglabs.github.io/learn-harness-engineering/en/lectures/lecture-09-why-agents-declare-victory-too-early/)

### What this says about Lawmind specifically

**The LCC/RCC split IS the dual-control mechanism**, and it is measurably the
strongest one available. RCC catching this lane is not evidence the arrangement
is failing — **it is the arrangement working, and the 3% figure is what it buys.**
The failure would be RCC *not* catching it.

**But it is currently accidental, and the research says make it structural.** Every
one of this lane's errors was checkable by one command that was not run:

| the claim | the command that would have killed it |
| --- | --- |
| *"the bus works, it has been used"* | `ls .agents/bus/.cursor-*` — no file had ever been written |
| *"resolution will reach 49.1%"* | the `--apply` itself; 16,790 were self-citations |
| *"landed and applied to production"* | one unauthenticated request; 401 vs 404 |
| *"13,834 edges are an extraction defect"* | reading the line in `citations-cli.ts` that writes them |
| *"build saved searches"* | reading 13 lines further down the contract |
| *"these guards run nowhere"* | `grep check- scripts/ci-local.mjs` |

**Six errors, one shape: a claim about system state, checkable in one command,
asserted instead.** Not reasoning failures — verification failures.

### The change this justifies

`CLAUDE.md`'s `DONE:/VERIFY:` artifact is a **natural-language summary**, and
ECLoop's ablation found structured evidence conditions **outperform exactly
that**. The proposal, for the founder's decision rather than a silent edit:

> **`VERIFY:` must name a COMMAND and its expected output, never a description.**
> "probe the running service" is a description. `curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' …` returning `401` is a condition.

And the cheap-detector finding argues for **more guard scripts, not more prose** —
`scripts/check-*.mjs` caught three real defects today at a cost of milliseconds,
which is the 72%-vs-13% result reproduced in miniature.

## 2 · CITATION SEARCH ACCURACY — the competition is worse than we assumed

### The number that should shape our marketing and our gate

Stanford RegLab's *Hallucination-Free?* study, published in the **Journal of
Empirical Legal Studies**, tested the paid, RAG-backed legal research products:

| product | hallucination rate |
| --- | --- |
| **Lexis+ AI** | **17%** |
| **Westlaw AI-Assisted Research** | **33%** |
| GPT-4, unassisted | 43% |

Systems with **multi-layer validation** reach **<1%**.
[Stanford RegLab paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20362) ·
[what the science says](https://www.ailawlibrarians.com/2026/02/19/what-the-science-says-about-hallucinations-in-legal-research/)

**Three consequences for us.**

1. **The harness is the product.** A third of Westlaw's AI answers carry a
   citation defect. Our three-field, three-tier design is not over-engineering
   against a solved problem — it is the thing nobody has solved.
2. **"Multi-layer validation" is what we already call Tier 1/2/3.** The <1%
   figure is the target and it is reachable, which is worth knowing before
   anyone proposes a shortcut through it.
3. **The errors are not only fabrications.** The study counts *real citations
   with mischaracterised holdings* and *correct quotes with the wrong procedural
   posture*. **Our harness checks existence, not characterisation.** That is a
   genuine blind spot in our own design and it is not currently on any list.

### Retrieval technique — what the evidence supports

| finding | implication for us |
| --- | --- |
| On legal passage retrieval, **BM25 and dense embeddings differ by 0.3 pp** (37.1% vs 36.8%) | **The embedding is not the lever.** This is the single most important finding for the GPU question — see §4 |
| Hybrid **BM25 + dense, fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion**, then cross-encoder rerank, is the production standard | We already run hybrid; the RRF-then-cross-encoder ordering is worth checking against what `retrieve.ts` does |
| Dense retrieval's effectiveness on legal case retrieval *"remains limited"* — long documents, precise lexical matching of statutes and facts | Argues against spending GPU-months to embed 20M documents |

Sources: [hybrid search reference](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/hybrid-search-bm25-vector-reranking-reference-2026) ·
[BM25 to Corrective RAG benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01733v1) ·
[self-evolving agent for legal case retrieval](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.17220)

### §3a · CORRECTION 11 Aug 2026, LCC — the eSCR row above was a false claim, caught by actually fetching it

**`digiscr.sci.gov.in` does not resolve.** `curl`, direct DNS, both fail —
`getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND`. This citation was carried into `CURRENT_PLAN.md`,
`TECHNICAL_INVENTORY.md` and `AGENT_BROWSER.md` from this document without
anyone fetching it first, three times over. It is the exact failure pattern
§1 of this same document describes — a plausible-sounding claim, never run
through the one command that would have killed it.

**The real site, found via `sci.gov.in`'s own homepage: `https://scr.sci.gov.in/scrsearch/`.**
Reachable (HTTP 200), fetched and read directly, 11 Aug 2026. Two things
change the recommendation, not one:

1. **It is CAPTCHA-gated** (`securimage`, the same widget eCourts uses — the
   markup literally ships `ecourts.js`/`ecourts.select.min.js`, so this is
   built on the eCourts platform, a different portal from it). `CLAUDE.md`'s
   CAPTCHA-bypass permission is scoped to `services/api/src/court/ecourts.ts`
   under the eCourts registrar's specific written grant — it does **not**
   extend to this domain. Automating it would be exactly the "new source,
   normal provenance/authorization process" case `CLAUDE.md` §6a carves out,
   not a free pass.
2. **Even ignoring the CAPTCHA, it cannot do the job it was cited for.** Its
   search form has three modes — free-text keyword, **S.C.R.** citation
   (`citation_year`/`vol`/`supl`/`page`), and **neutral citation**
   (`neu_citation_year`/`ncn`/`neu_no`, prefixed `INSC`). **Zero occurrences of
   "SCC" or "AIR" anywhere on the page.** Both of those are formats we already
   hold natively — 38,342 of 38,342 judgments carry S.C.R., 38,214 carry a
   neutral citation (§4 above). The unresolved edges are **SCC/AIR-cited**
   (4,485 of 4,489, `AUTHORITY_COVERAGE.md`), and this tool has no field to
   search by either. **It cannot resolve the concordance gap it was proposed
   to solve, independent of the access question.**

**Corrected recommendation:** eSCR is not a free substitute for the licensed
SCC/AIR↔S.C.R. concordance `FOUNDER_QUEUE.md` already has open. It is at most
a human-paced verification cross-check for a judgment we already hold by
S.C.R. or neutral citation — the same category as `judgments.ecourts.gov.in`
below, and subject to the same CAPTCHA constraint. Not pursued further.

### The one to refuse

**`eCourtsIndia.com` appears in search results as an "open dataset" of SC/HC/
district judgments.** `CLAUDE.md` §6 already forbids it by name and the reason
stands: **its access was never authorised, and buying it launders someone else's
offence into our corpus.** Recorded here because it will keep surfacing in
searches and looks legitimate.

**ILDC** (35k SC cases) is *"available for research and non-commercial purposes
by request"* — **non-commercial excludes us.** Not usable.

## 4 · THE AWS CORPUS, THE GPU AND THE eCOURTS GRANT

### What is actually true today, by query, not by memory

| | |
| --- | --- |
| High Court documents enumerated on AWS | **20,529,202** all years · **15,771,566** in the decade |
| High Court judgments in `judgments` | **0** |
| Supreme Court judgments held | 38,341, of which **38,214 carry a neutral citation** |
| HC citation pass, right now | **263,783 documents read, 2016 only**, 40,166 citations, 42.5 docs/s |
| Extraction cost, measured | 186 ms/PDF → **4.2 days on 8 workers** |
| Embedding cost, from `DATASETS.md` | **~3,956 GPU-hours** for a High Court decade |

### The two questions blocking it are still the founder's — but one has changed

`CURRENT_PLAN.md` §Q2 states them: **citability** and **embedding cost**.

**On citability — new information.** The blocker was: *"Neither AWS metadata
variant has a citation column, so a High Court judgment from that bucket is
searchable and not citable."* That is true of the **metadata**. It is not true of
the **text**: from 2023, High Courts print a neutral citation **inside the
judgment**. So for 2023 onward the citation is extractable and the judgment is
citable without any metadata column.

**This does not resolve §Q2 and I have not treated it as resolved.** It narrows
it: the question becomes *"ingest 2023+ where citability is solved, or hold out
for the older years too."* That is still a scope decision.

**Acted on immediately, because it was time-critical:** `extractCitations` had
**no pattern for the High Court neutral-citation form**. The pass has read 2016
only, so 2023+ is still ahead — but it records every document it reads including
the empty ones, and never re-reads them. The blind spot would have been permanent
and silent. **Pattern added, tested, before the pass reaches those years.**
Format taken from the courts' own circulars, **not yet observed in our data**.

**On the GPU — the evidence says do not start.** §2's finding is decisive:
**BM25 and dense embeddings differ by 0.3 pp on legal passage retrieval**, and
dense retrieval's effectiveness on legal case retrieval *"remains limited"*.
Spending ~3,956 GPU-hours to embed a decade of High Court judgments buys the
smaller half of a hybrid system whose lexical half is already built.

**The GPU has a better job**, and it is one nothing else can do: **the
pseudonymiser** (FQ-D1). That is the single blocker on core feature #3, it is
NER inference rather than corpus embedding, and OpenNyAI's Indian-legal NER
(§3) is the baseline `PRIVACY_PII.md` asks for and we do not have.

### The eCourts grant — what it is and is not for

The registrar's grant (to **January 2029**) authorises **bulk cause-list
harvesting** and permits CAPTCHA bypass **only** in
`services/api/src/court/ecourts.ts`, **only** while the grant is unexpired.
`CLAUDE.md` §6.

**It is not a judgment-corpus source and was never the plan.** The corpus comes
from AWS Open Data under CC-BY-4.0; the grant powers the **hearing briefing** —
Tier B, the daily loop, which ships before the library. Tier 3 per-citation
confirmation stays a human solving a CAPTCHA and vouching, and the test asserting
`citations/verify.ts` holds no HTTP client stays.

**Bulk resolution writes `verified_by_source = 'ecourts_bulk'`, never
`'ecourts'`** — a machine may not wear a human's badge.
