# GOING TO MARKET IN INDIA — distribution, the lay user, and the tools

Researched 8 August 2026. **`docs/COMPETITIVE.md` holds pricing and the launch
offer. It contains no distribution plan at all** — nothing about how an advocate
first hears of us. This file is that gap, and it exists because of one
observation the founder made on the ground that no desk research had produced.

> **Supreme Today has salespeople who walk court to court, find the buyer, and
> activate the plan on the customer's phone then and there.**

That is not a marketing tactic. It is the whole answer to how a ₹20,000/year
product reaches a solo practitioner who will never search an app store for it,
and `docs/COMPETITOR_SUPREME_TODAY.md` §4 has a number that only makes sense once
you know it: **50,000+ installs against 10 ratings.** People are not finding that
app. They are being sold it, in person, and then not opening it.

---

## 1 · Why the corridor works, and what it is actually selling

**Advocates are the most physically concentrated professional buyer in India.**
~**2.01 million** enrolled advocates (Ministry of Law & Justice, Rajya Sabha
reply) — Uttar Pradesh **400,016**, Maharashtra & Goa **191,394**, Delhi
**149,655** — working out of **1,000+ district and subordinate court complexes**.
A district court bar room is the same few hundred people, in the same building,
every working day.

Three things follow, and only the third is about software.

**Trust transfers sideways, not downwards.** A senior in the bar room saying "I
use it" outsells any claim we could make. Legal publishing in India has always
been sold this way — through court-premises booksellers and bar contacts.
*Ronit Law Agency* lists Supreme Today's products, which is a bookseller
distributing software.

**The close is on the buyer's phone, and that removes the real obstacle.** The
obstacle to a ₹20,000 purchase was never the price — `COMPETITIVE.md` establishes
that a solo advocate already spends ₹30,000–50,000/year on research. It is that
"I'll sign up later" means never. Activation in the corridor eliminates the gap
between deciding and paying.

**What is actually being sold is relief from a specific fear.** After
*Pooja Ramesh Singh* (2026 INSC 668) that fear has a name and a penalty. A
salesperson does not have to explain retrieval quality. They have to say: *the
Supreme Court called it misconduct in July, and here is the thing that stops it
happening to you.*

## 2 · Four channels, in the order they should be tried

Ordered by cost-to-first-customer, cheapest first. None needs a sales force to
start.

### 2a · The bar association, not the advocate

Every district court complex has a bar association with elected office-bearers,
a bar room, a noticeboard and a WhatsApp group. It is the single trust node that
reaches a few hundred advocates at once, and it is **already the unit Supreme
Today's field sales has to work through one advocate at a time.**

An institutional arrangement at a per-member rate reaches the whole bar without
walking the corridor, and it converts our weakest position — an unknown brand —
into the association's recommendation. It also fits `PD-2`: **enrolment number is
captured, never gates access**, so verifying membership is a bar-association
matter and not a barrier we impose.

**The asymmetry worth naming:** Supreme Today's field model costs them a salary
per territory. An association deal costs one conversation and scales to the next
association by reputation. They cannot easily copy it — a publisher selling
₹20,000 individual subscriptions will not undercut itself with a bulk rate.

### 2b · The advocate's clerk — the node nobody sells to

Every chamber of any size has a clerk who keeps the diary, checks the cause list,
files, and tells the advocate where to be tomorrow. **That is precisely the Tier B
daily loop.** `PRODUCT_BRIEF.md` says the loop creates the habit; the clerk is the
person whose entire job is that loop, and no competitor's product is aimed at
them — `docs/ASO.md` §1 found **none of the five Play-listed competitors leads
with a listing, a hearing, or a next date.**

Win the clerk and the chamber follows, because the clerk chooses the tool that
makes their day shorter. This is the cheapest wedge we have and it is unclaimed.

### 2c · Law students, before the budget exists

Students become advocates and arrive with a habit already formed. A free student
tier costs little (they generate few briefings — no listed matters) and buys the
next cohort. Supreme Today has no reason to do this: a publisher monetises
subscriptions, and free students are pure cost to them. To us they are the
cheapest possible acquisition of a 40-year customer.

### 2d · Tele-Law and the CSC network — for the lay user, §3

The Department of Justice already runs the last mile. **Tele-Law**, under the
**DISHA** scheme: **50 lakh legal consultations delivered**, expanding from 1,800
Common Service Centres to **all 2.65 lakh Gram Panchayats**, with a stated target
of **one crore beneficiaries**. Village Level Entrepreneurs at CSCs connect
citizens to panel lawyers.

**We should not compete with this. We should be useful to it.** The founder has
already participated in one government scheme — the eCourts data grant running to
January 2029 — so the relationship and the credibility exist. A tool that helps a
CSC operator or a panel lawyer explain a procedure correctly is aligned with the
scheme's own objective, not competing for its users.

## 3 · The lay user — and the line that must not be crossed

The founder's examples are exactly right about demand: *how do I recover money I
lent a friend* · *how do I fight someone illegally occupying my land*. Millions of
people have a legal problem, no idea of the pathway, and no way to ask.

**But this segment carries a regulatory risk the advocate product does not, and
it is the first thing to get right.**

### What the rules actually say

**BCI Rule 36** (Bar Council of India Rules, 1975) prohibits an advocate from
soliciting work or advertising *"directly or indirectly, whether by circulars,
advertisements, **touts**, personal communications…"*. In 2024–25 the BCI
directed State Bar Councils to withdraw violating advertisements, **banned the
use of influencers to promote legal services**, and **warned digital platforms**
they risk being drawn into formal complaints. A formal complaint has been filed
with the BCI against **Vakilsearch** for soliciting and advertising legal
services.

Consultation platforms survive on a specific structure: they present as **neutral
connectors** where the advocate–client relationship begins only on engagement,
verify advocates against the State Bar Council roll, and require a current
Certificate of Practice. That structure is a defence, not an immunity — the
complaint above exists anyway.

**And the safe ground is clearly marked.** Publishing educational material —
explaining legal concepts, rights and procedure — **is widely treated as distinct
from prohibited advertising.**

### The recommendation, and it is narrow on purpose

**Build the explainer. Do not build the marketplace.**

A lay user asks *"my friend has not returned ₹2 lakh"* and gets **the pathway,
sourced**: a demand notice; whether a cheque was involved and what s. 138 of the
Negotiable Instruments Act then means; a summary suit under Order XXXVII CPC; the
limitation period; which court, by pecuniary jurisdiction; what documents matter.
Every step traceable to a statute or a judgment in our own corpus, rendered from
the database row like every other citation.

**Three hard lines:**

1. **No routing to a named advocate, ever.** The moment we introduce a user to a
   lawyer we are a connector, and connectors attract BCI complaints. This also
   protects the advocate product: we cannot credibly sell to 2 million advocates
   while competing with them for their clients.
2. **Procedure and law, never advice on outcome.** *"This is the process for a
   summary suit"* is information. *"You will win"* or *"you do not need a lawyer"*
   is not, and would be both wrong and dangerous.
3. **It ends by saying: take this to an advocate.** Which is true, is the correct
   advice, and is the single best thing we could say to 2 million advocates about
   why they should trust us.

### Why this is worth doing even though it earns nothing directly

It is **demand-pull into the advocate product**. A client who walks in saying
*"Lawmind says this is an Order XXXVII suit"* has just recommended us to an
advocate, for free, at the exact moment that advocate is deciding how to proceed.
Nothing in a corridor sale is as persuasive as a client bringing your name in.

It is also the answer to Supreme Today's field sales that does not require a
field sales force. They sell one advocate at a time. **A lay-facing product sells
to the advocate through their own clients**, and a 58-year-old publisher will not
follow us there: it is not their business, and their brand is built on being the
profession's, not the public's.

> **This is an ask, not a plan.** `PRODUCT_BRIEF.md`'s rule is explicit — if what
> you are about to build does not serve one of the four features, stop and ask. A
> lay-facing explainer serves none of them. It is in `docs/FOUNDER_QUEUE.md`.

**Firecrawl is AGPL-3.0, and `CLAUDE.md` §OSS FIRST says "MIT/Apache/BSD ok. AGPL
is NOT."** The distinction that matters, stated precisely rather than as a flat
refusal:

- **Self-hosting or embedding it: no.** AGPL §13 reaches software offered over a
  network, which is exactly what we are.
- **Their hosted cloud API: legally fine** — consuming somebody's API is not
  distributing their software, and the AGPL obligation sits with the operator.
  But it is a **paid external vendor**, and `CLAUDE.md` says ask before adding
  one. It also puts our corpus pipeline behind somebody else's rate limit and
  pricing.
- **Crawl4AI is the direct Apache-2.0 substitute** and does the same job.

**The tool is not the decision. The guard is.** Whatever we use, the binding
constraint is ours and already built:

- Every eCourts fetch goes through `services/api/src/court/ecourts.ts` — the only
  module permitted an HTTP client — and through `decide()`, which enforces the
  rate limiter and the kill switch **before** any request.
- Every request writes `ecourts_fetch_ledger`, refusals included, so *"did we stay
  inside the grant"* is answerable by query. `court/ledger-rollback.test.ts` now
  proves an unrecorded fetch cannot produce usable data.
- The grant's CAPTCHA bypass is scoped to bulk cause-list harvesting and expires
  with the grant in **January 2029**.

**A scraper that reaches a court website without passing `decide()` is a breach of
the authorisation, whatever its licence.** Any of these tools is acceptable *as
the transport inside* that module; none may be given its own path to the network.
That constraint is what `guard.test.ts`'s source-level sweep already enforces, and
adding a crawler is the exact moment it earns its keep.

**AWS Open Data needs none of them.** `DATA_ADVANTAGE.md` §2e: 17.8M judgments,
25 High Courts, CC-BY-4.0, in S3, no AWS account required. That is `aws s3 sync`,
not a crawler. **Reach for a scraper only where there is no bulk source** —
tribunals and the district tail — and remember that the tribunal APIs found so
far are barred scraper-resellers (`CLAUDE.md` §6).

**Manus and similar agents:** useful for one-off research and for reading a page
a parser chokes on. They must never sit in an ingest path — an agent that decides
what a judgment says is exactly the model-commentary source `DATASETS.md`
forbids, and it cannot be made to pass the fetch ledger.

## 5 · What I am NOT claiming

- **No primary source for the field-sales model.** §1 rests on the founder's
  ground observation plus circumstantial support (bookseller distribution, the
  50K-installs-vs-10-ratings gap). It is the most valuable input in this document
  and it is one person's observation — worth confirming with two or three more
  advocates before hiring anyone.
- **No bar association has been approached**, and I do not know what they charge,
  whether they can contract, or who decides. That is the first thing to find out
  and it costs one phone call.
- **Tele-Law partnership is a hypothesis.** The scheme is real and the numbers
  are the Department of Justice's own; whether they take private tooling is
  unknown.
- **The lay-user regulatory read is mine, not counsel's.** The rules and the
  enforcement pattern are quoted accurately; whether our specific design clears
  them is a question for the lawyer who is already owed a written residency view
  (OD-2). **Do not ship a lay-facing surface on my reading alone.**
- **No cost model for any channel.** A per-member bar rate and a field salesperson
  have very different unit economics and neither is computed here.
